Four visual worlds for an international health-travel platform

The same hero, the same case-file pages, the same buttons, in English and Arabic, built four ways. Every colour pair below was measured against WCAG AA (4.5:1 for text). Nothing here invents claims: the pages say what the platform produces, the case number is marked as an example. Look at it on your phone as well as a laptop.

What it says

"Exact, modern, nothing to hide." The register of the newest Saudi and Emirati health brands; speaks to younger families and to payers.

Where it wins

  • Highest contrast and the cleanest staff screens of the four.
  • Kufi headlines make the Arabic site the better-looking one.
  • Mint sits comfortably beside the logo's teal.

Where it risks

  • Can feel cold to a frightened patient; warmth must come from photographs of real people and the copy.
  • Black-and-white with one accent is a common startup look; the Kufi voice and the stamp carry the distinctiveness, so they are non-negotiable.

Cost to adopt

Medium. Token re-point without a logo change; the Arabic type scale must be rebuilt for Kufi; three to four sessions.

Recommendation

Adopt B, Petrol Night, for the openings and keep Petrol & Paper for everything else. It is the one move that makes the first screen unmistakable, keeps the logo, keeps the Arabic legibility where it matters (every working page stays light), and costs one or two sessions because every colour already exists in the token file. The dossier pages become the brightest thing on a dark desk, which is the whole idea of the brand made visible.

If you want a true re-brand rather than an evolution, C, Nile Ink & Limestone is the strongest candidate: it carries the "official, international" signal better than any teal can. But it only works if the hand-heart mark is recoloured to ink and copper, and that is the brand decision you asked me to treat as settled. D is the modern alternative if your growth bet is the Gulf and younger families; it is the coldest for the Libyan and Sudanese family on a phone at night.

How to decide in ten minutes. Open this page on your phone outdoors and indoors; read the Arabic hero of each world aloud; ask one person from the target countries which of the four they would send their mother's reports to, and why. Then tell me A, B, C or D.

  1. B chosen: I apply the night opening to the home hero, the final panel and the Keycloak login, with the rule "no gold, no gradients", in the next UX session.
  2. C or D chosen: I write the token proposal (every hex above), the Arabic type scale, and the logo question for C, and we run it as UX plan area 0 before any other area.
  3. A chosen: nothing changes; the UX plan proceeds as written.
